# DuoStage Content Strategy

## Product truth

DuoStage is not a quiz app. The content unit is a **social tension reveal**.

The best clip has four beats:

1. **Claim** — both players understand the dilemma immediately.
2. **Commit** — each side chooses before seeing the other.
3. **Reveal** — the hinge exposes whether they match.
4. **Argument** — the video ends, but the comments keep going.

A prompt is valuable when the viewer has an opinion before the reveal.

## Viral prompt formula

Prioritize prompts with three properties:

- **Binary clarity**: a viewer can answer in one second.
- **Value collision**: both answers are defensible.
- **Relationship consequence**: the answer says something about trust, money, status, loyalty, privacy, taste or authenticity.

Score candidate prompts 0–2 on each:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instant comprehension | needs explanation | mostly clear | obvious in <2s |
| Split potential | one obvious answer | mild split | strong legitimate split |
| Reveal reaction | little consequence | amusing | changes how players see each other |
| Comment hook | nothing to add | some opinions | viewers must pick a side |
| Tag/replay potential | personal only | shareable | "I need to ask X this" |

Ship prompts scoring **8/10+**. Hero-demo prompts should score **9/10+**.

## Heat system

### 🌶 Warm
Low-risk disagreement. Good for onboarding and broad audiences.

Examples: first-date bill etiquette, outfit repeating, allowance for chores.

### 🌶🌶 Spicy — default launch deck
Strong social norms with two defensible answers.

Examples: phone privacy, live location, friends with an ex, ghosting, salary on a first date, parents posting children online, heavy beauty filters.

### 🌶🌶🌶 Chaos — opt-in only, not default launch content
Questions that can expose painful relationship asymmetry or create lasting conflict.

Examples: who settled, who is more replaceable, who would cheat, who loves the other less, private sexual history, humiliating financial secrets.

P0 does **not** ship Chaos prompts. If added later, they require an explicit opt-in warning and separate deck.

## Repeatable prompt formats

### 1. Pick a side
Both sides answer the same controversial question.

`Should couples share location 24/7?`

Best for comments because viewers can answer too.

### 2. Predict them
A answers their own position; B predicts A, then reverse in another round.

`Would A let a partner read their phone?`

Best for revealing how well people know each other.

### 3. Who's more likely
Both secretly choose A or B.

`Who would disappear from the group after getting into a relationship?`

Best for friend-group reactions.

### 4. Dealbreaker
Each chooses `dealbreaker / not a dealbreaker`.

`Still close friends with an ex.`

Best for dating/street interviews.

### 5. Status reversal
Ask a question where the reveal can unexpectedly make one participant look stricter, cheaper, more jealous, more private or more open-minded than expected.

Best for thumbnails and reaction cuts.

## End-card comment hooks

The exported video should not end only with `MATCH 100%`.

Use a short debate hook matched to the prompt:

- `WHO'S RIGHT?`
- `RED FLAG OR NORMAL?`
- `PRIVACY OR SECRECY?`
- `FAIR OR CHEAP?`
- `WOULD YOU ALLOW IT?`
- `PICK A SIDE ↓`

This is part of the video format, not caption copy.

## Safety / brand guardrails

Do not manufacture controversy from protected characteristics, tragedy, self-harm, abuse, illegal behavior, sexual coercion, medical information or doxxing-style private facts.

Avoid prompts whose entertainment value depends on humiliating a participant. The tension should come from **competing norms**, not from cruelty.

For minors, keep prompts about general family rules and privacy norms; do not ask children to disclose secrets, romantic/sexual information, family finances or allegations against adults.

## Launch content thesis

The default deck should feel like:

> "This question is dangerous enough that I need to know my partner's answer — but safe enough that I'll still press record."

That is the target line for DuoStage content.
